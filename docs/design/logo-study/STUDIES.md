# Mluva logo study

Ten independent concepts, each critiqued and revised before generation. The production mark remains unchanged until Daniel chooses a direction.

## Brimming Tide

[Open the Figma study](https://www.figma.com/design/4mdtod74gknaCCm8q1nrDj?node-id=151-14)

### App identity

Mluva is a small, capable place to turn speaking into useful text without breaking the rhythm of work. Its Czech name makes speech the centre of the identity; its Omarchy home gives that identity a practical, literate setting. The promise, ‘Most delightful dictation for Omarchy’, should feel plausible through immediate interaction, calm presentation and careful handling of what the person actually said. Original recognition, edited source and rewrites remain distinct, while Markdown makes the result something the person can continue shaping. Local and cloud providers are independent choices, so the identity should suggest personal agency without implying that every operation stays on the device. Brimming Tide expresses the moment before a thought becomes a sentence: there is already substance, and speaking gives it a usable form. ‘A little more delight every day’ belongs to that modest improvement in everyday flow.

### Mark identity

The mark is one solid body with the familiar directional cue of speech and the upper contour of quiet water. A generous, rounded mass holds most of the visual weight. Its top gently dips and rises once, like a meniscus responding to breath; a short, broad tail grows from the lower right. These two decisions carry the meaning without interior symbols. The body is the thought gathered and retained, while the tail makes it addressed and expressible. Water also connects naturally to the existing film language and Omarchy’s fluid movement, but the sign must remain intelligible away from that film. At first glance it should register as a calm speech form; the water reading rewards a second look. The slight asymmetry supplies the organic quality Daniel likes in the existing breathing artwork. The geometry stays controlled enough for a launcher, panel or monochrome stamp, with neither a literal vessel nor an illustration of a wave.

### Logo design

Draw one closed, completely filled silhouette: a broad speech body with a gently tidal top edge and a small tail at the lower right. Its overall proportions are approximately 1.22 units wide to one unit tall, including the tail. The principal body is a softened, slightly horizontal oval whose lower half is full and settled. Its sides turn into the base with generous continuous curvature. Keep the greatest width near the vertical middle, and let the underside reach its lowest body point just left of centre, so the mass appears held rather than propelled. The contour must feel deliberately drawn, never mechanically wobbled. Across the upper edge, move from the rounded left shoulder into one shallow dip just right of centre, then rise into a broad right shoulder. This is one slow surface movement, not a sequence of ripples. The total vertical excursion of this meniscus should be about twelve percent of the body height: enough to survive reduction, but too restrained to resemble a breaking wave. The right shoulder merges directly into the side; it has no hook, pointed crest or projecting beak. A conventional speech tail begins in the lower right quarter. Give it a broad root, approximately eighteen percent of body width, and a short downward reach, approximately fourteen percent of body height. Its gently blunted tip leans right but stays within the body’s rightmost extent. A shallow concave return makes the tail legible without pinching the body into a neck. The tail must read as a speech pointer, not a water drop or separate appendage. Use a single, opaque ink fill, with no internal holes, divisions, outlines or floating parts. The water cue belongs entirely to the boundary of the same mass that signifies speech. On a 24 px canvas, the tail should retain roughly a two-pixel projection and the surface movement roughly two pixels of vertical difference. Optical adjustment at this size may broaden the tail root, but must preserve the same silhouette. Present the master alone on real transparency, with generous clear space. Its primary success test is speech recognition in a small monochrome silhouette; its secondary success is the quiet sensation of a thought brimming into words.

### Wordmark design

Set the exact word ‘Mluva’ from JetBrains Mono Medium, then sculpt the resulting filled letter shapes as a restrained piece of brand artwork. The application’s editable type remains the unchanged JetBrains Mono family. Retain the capital M, lowercase l, u, v and double-storey a, the family’s characteristic construction, its upright stance and its measured technical rhythm. The word should still be recognisably related to a terminal, with a little of the tide mark’s ease. Keep every letter on one stable baseline and retain the original x-height and ascender relationship. Begin with the existing principle of a narrowed l. Reduce its advance and horizontal reach, preserving enough top and foot structure to distinguish it from an uppercase I or an isolated rule. Close the visual gap after M so the first two letters belong to one name; leave enough separation before u to keep the l’s foot out of the bowl. Adjust the remaining pairs optically, keeping u–v slightly more open than a purely numerical spacing system would suggest. The distinctive sculpting lives low in the word. Give the u’s bottom turn a subtly fuller exterior curve while keeping its inner counter open and structurally faithful to JetBrains Mono. Soften the v’s external apex by a tiny amount to balance its apparent weight with that bowl; preserve the unmistakable V geometry. Round only the most exposed outer corners by a radius no larger than one tenth of stem thickness. Leave the counters and major terminals crisp enough for small use. The a keeps its double-storey form and compact finish; do not turn its terminal into a wave, tail or decorative exit. All five letters remain separate. No ligature, watery cutout or fluctuating baseline is needed. Their shared lower weight and small optical corrections establish the relationship with the filled speech body. Produce one opaque monochrome wordmark on real transparency, without a symbol or descriptor. It should read instantly as Mluva before the viewer notices that the lettering has been tuned.

### Self-critique

**subject:** Logo

**initialDescription:** The initial mark is a single pool of dark ink shaped into a low, organic speech vessel. Start with a broad, rounded body, about one and a half times wider than tall, with a substantial curved underside. Its top is a visible water surface rather than a straight rim: a shallow swell on the left runs into a central trough and then rises on the right. At that right edge, the wave lifts slightly above the main mass and curls inward before returning to the side of the body. The inward return creates a small crescent of transparent air that suggests the pressure of an imminent word. A short triangular speech tail descends from the lower left, balancing the elevated crest on the opposite side. The body, crest and tail are fused into one opaque form; no drawn outline or detached decoration is present. The design describes a thought that has accumulated and is just beginning to overflow into expression. Its visual energy runs from the heavy, settled base to the light, raised edge. The water should feel slow and tactile, with the soft continuity of the existing breathing artwork. Shape the left side more generously than the right, giving the mass an almost held-in-the-palm character. Keep the small crescent open to the outside, never enclosed as an eye or an interior counter. At large size, the uneven surface offers the recognisable fluid detail; at small size, the speech tail provides the category cue. Use only one flat ink colour, preserve a truly transparent background, and surround the isolated form with generous clear space. The ideal emotional reading is a reservoir of thought finding a voice: sufficient substance, easy release, no strain. The mark must work as a monochrome launcher symbol and as the still endpoint of a slow breathing animation. It should also sit over the film’s water footage without needing a new illustrative style.

**adversarialCritique:** The curled crest and opposing tail can read as a whale or a bird. The metaphor is asking viewers to decode too much before they reach speech.

The narrow transparent crescent is likely to close at 24 px, leaving an unexplained lump or accidental eye. It also recreates the old mark’s problem of depending on abstract negative space.

A body one and a half times wider than tall becomes too thin inside a square launcher allocation. The water character may survive while the speech cue loses weight.

Overflow implies leaking or losing information. That is a poor secondary association for a product that keeps original recognition separate from revisions.

A lifting crest promises drama. The product’s delight is quieter, and the existing film already supplies actual moving water.

**decision:** Revise once: remove the curling crest and crescent, compress the proportions, move the speech tail to the lower right, and contain the water cue within a broad meniscus across the top.

**subject:** Wordmark

**initialDescription:** The initial wordmark begins with the exact text Mluva in JetBrains Mono Medium and turns the lower half of the name into a restrained water rhythm. Preserve the capital M and the familiar lowercase sequence, but redraw the bottoms of u, v and a with a shared softness. The M remains the firm architectural opening. Narrow the l and tighten its approach to u so that the beginning of the name feels compact and intentional. Give u a deeper, fuller lower bowl, then round the bottom of v until its apex feels almost like the next small trough. Extend the a’s lower right terminal a little beyond its normal boundary and let it rise gently, echoing the mark’s lifted crest. The changes should be made to the filled glyph shapes, with no added decorative strokes. Keep the original JetBrains Mono x-height, ascender height and approximate stem weight. The normal monospaced advance may be loosened into optical spacing for the brand artwork, while all editable interface typography continues to use the actual family. Allow the u to sit just perceptibly below the common baseline, as round letters conventionally overshoot it, and let the extended a finish the word with a quiet upward motion. The intention is for the sequence uva to feel like three phases of one slow ripple without becoming a connected script. Retain an open u counter and the double-storey a. Use one flat, opaque ink colour and a genuine transparent background, with no separate logo or tagline. The result should combine the confidence of software typography with a small physical impression of water: grounded at the beginning, gently moving at the end.

**adversarialCritique:** The rippling uva sequence is too literal. It creates a second water illustration inside a word that should primarily name the product.

Deepening u and heavily rounding v can erase the family’s precise rhythm and make the letters look melted. That weakens the Omarchy and JetBrains Mono relationship.

An extended rising a terminal resembles a flourish or a smile. It can distract from the final letter and makes the name feel friendlier than the product’s calm, capable tone requires.

Tightening l toward u without protecting their silhouettes can make the pair read as a joined glyph at small size.

The proposed wave relies on a moving baseline. Even a slight visible bounce would make the lettering less stable beside the already asymmetric mark.

**decision:** Revise once: remove the terminal flourish and baseline movement, retain the source letter structures, and concentrate all softness in small exterior optical corrections to u and v.

### Revision

**logo:** The final description replaces the illustrative wave curl with one shallow meniscus and removes every internal void. Overall width narrows from roughly 1.5 to 1.22 times height. A broad, short speech tail moves to the lower right and remains inside the body’s rightmost extent, reducing animal and spilling associations. The body now holds its visual substance while the speech cue provides the meaning.

**wordmark:** The final description preserves a common baseline, conventional double-storey a and separate letter shapes. It removes the a flourish, avoids a melted v, and limits curve changes to the lower exterior of u and a tiny optical easing of v. The narrowed l and optical pair spacing carry most of the customisation, preserving a clear JetBrains Mono lineage.

**status:** One initial concept and one revision completed for the mark and wordmark. The final descriptions and production prompts specify the revised direction. This is a study; no production brand asset is changed.

### Small-size risks

At 24 px, a meniscus shallower than about two rendered pixels may look like an ordinary speech bubble. Keep one visible dip and one rise without adding finer wave detail.

A narrow tail root can alias into a detached fleck. Preserve a broad connection and at least a roughly two-pixel projection beyond the body at the target size.

An exaggerated right shoulder can become a beak. It must turn directly into the flank and never extend beyond the main body’s width.

A long tail and a low body may read as an animal. Keep the final compact proportions and put the tail below the body rather than behind it.

The l foot can crowd u in the wordmark. Verify the name as Mluva at small text size; prefer a little more spacing over further letter modification.

Ink and reversed light fills may appear to have different weights. Any later reversal should use the same master first, with only evidence-led optical compensation.

### Optical rules

Use one connected filled mark, one opaque colour and genuine transparent alpha outside it; no contour strokes or internal negative spaces.

Target an overall mark width-to-height ratio of 1.22:1, including the lower-right speech tail.

Limit the top to one shallow dip and one broad rise, with about twelve percent of body height between their extrema.

Keep the upper-right rise and the tail tip inside the body’s rightmost extent; neither becomes a projecting beak or arrow.

Keep the tail root near eighteen percent of body width and its downward reach near fourteen percent of body height; resolve those values optically at 24 px.

Centre the visible body optically, allowing for the lower-right tail’s pull; do not mechanically centre only the bounding rectangle.

Preserve a level wordmark baseline, JetBrains Mono Medium structure, separate glyphs, double-storey a and open counters.

Narrow l and adjust spacing before changing letter geometry; soften u and v only enough to equalise apparent weight.

Keep exposed-corner rounding below one tenth of the wordmark stem thickness and keep all fill opacity at one hundred percent.

Reserve at least one quarter of the mark’s body height as clear space on every side; use the symbol alone when a readable wordmark would not fit.

### Motion relationship

The existing 3.4-second breathing cadence can animate only the meniscus and a small amount of body fullness. Keep the lower mass almost still while the upper dip shallows and the right shoulder rises by a small amount, then returns. The tail remains recognisable and attached throughout, and the silhouette never sheds a droplet, spills, curls into an animal or rotates. A maximum contour displacement of roughly three percent of body height should be enough in a larger recorder treatment; the 24 px launcher uses the static master. The motion expresses relaxed readiness, not provider progress, accuracy or guaranteed local processing. For a title reveal, let the final filled silhouette settle before the unchanged wordmark appears. The mark can share the film’s water background through shape and timing without acquiring ripples, reflections or gradients. Reduced motion uses the steady revised silhouette. The wordmark does not wobble or breathe; its stable baseline gives the organic mark a dependable counterpart.

## Open Syllable

[Open the Figma study](https://www.figma.com/design/4mdtod74gknaCCm8q1nrDj?node-id=151-62)

### App identity

Mluva means speech or manner of speaking in Czech. It is an open-source dictation and rewriting app for Omarchy, designed to make speaking feel like an ordinary, pleasant part of working at a computer. A shortcut opens a brief space for speech; the result becomes editable text, with the original recognition retained alongside subsequent work. Local and cloud providers remain explicit choices. Exact source, Markdown, and reviewable rewrites give the calm surface a dependable foundation. This concept expresses the promise, 'Most delightful dictation for Omarchy', through an immediately recognizable place for speech. Its delight comes from the ease of forming and keeping a thought, rather than spectacle or a claim of perfect automation. The identity should sit comfortably beside terminal typography and changing desktop themes, then close a film with 'A little more delight every day'. This is a selection study; the existing production identity remains authoritative until Daniel chooses a direction.

### Mark identity

Open Syllable is a solid speech vessel whose upper edge opens into a deliberate central valley. The two shoulders and the valley suggest an M without placing a letter inside an icon. A short tail at the lower left supplies the primary reading: someone has spoken. The broad, connected lower body gives the thought somewhere to settle, while the open notch suggests that more can be said and the resulting text can still be shaped. This is an interpretation of speech and editability, not an encoding of a provider, privacy guarantee, or technical pipeline. The mark borrows the existing identity's generous filled curves, single-color confidence, and quiet movement, but adds a semantic anchor that survives without a written explanation. At a glance it should register as speech; on a second look the M rewards attention. There is one body, one opening, and one speaking tail. Its character is composed, receptive, and lightly human, with no facial expression.

### Logo design

Create one compact, filled speech vessel with an open central notch in its upper edge. Begin with a broad body, approximately 1.25 times wider than the body is tall. The sides are close to vertical through their middle sections, then turn through generous, continuous corners into a low, gently bowed base. This gives the silhouette a settled, useful presence. Avoid a perfect oval and avoid the domed twin lobes of a heart. The outer body should feel softly formed but deliberately drawn, with short, almost level shoulders on either side of the opening.

The opening is the distinguishing feature. From the inner end of each shoulder, an oblique edge descends towards a shallowly rounded central vertex. Together those edges cut a generous V into the upper half of the solid body. They expose the real transparent background rather than a white inset. The short upper shoulders, descending inner edges, and substantial outer flanks imply a capital M. Keep that inference subordinate to the speech vessel. Do not add an M glyph, another internal cutout, a mouth line, punctuation, or an inset symbol. Below the notch, leave a substantial connected mass; the mark must never look like a thin ribbon or an outlined chat bubble.

A single short tail grows directly from the lower-left quarter of the body and points down and slightly left. It is a broad triangular extension with a softly blunted tip, joined tangentially along its outer edge. Its inner return is concise enough to make the speech-bubble reading unmistakable. The tail occupies roughly one eighth of the total height and does not become a second lobe, a dangling stroke, or a long decorative flourish. The base beyond the tail remains broad and nearly level, preventing a heart reading.

Use a single opaque, very dark ink fill on real transparent alpha. The same geometry must support a light monochrome version later. Center its perceived mass, allowing for the tail, rather than merely centering its bounds. Retain calm continuous curvature, clear negative space, and enough interior substance for the 24-pixel launcher or panel view. The result should feel like a contemporary piece of purposeful software: speech offered a clear, comfortable shape. Generate only the isolated symbol, without a tile, lettering, border, surrounding composition, texture, lighting, gradient, or shadow.

### Wordmark design

Draw the exact word 'Mluva', with a capital M followed by lowercase l, u, v, and a. Use JetBrains Mono Medium as the structural starting point: its clear verticals, generous lowercase height, recognizable double-storey a, and matter-of-fact software character should remain visible. Convert the concept to custom filled lettering through controlled optical work. The word should read fluently at once and feel related to the symbol through proportions and curvature, rather than carrying a small speech bubble inside a letter.

Make the M slightly broader than its default cell. Keep the outer stems upright and give the central valley a small amount of additional depth, echoing the symbol's open upper notch without making an ornamental initial. The M should remain unmistakably typographic. Lightly soften only the tightest exterior corners, at a scale barely noticed in the finished word. Maintain the structural contrast between firm vertical sides and the open central gesture. Do not join the M to the next letter.

Narrow the l's allotted width while retaining its recognizable top flag and curved lower foot. This follows the existing identity's useful optical correction and avoids an empty monospaced interval in the middle of the name. Give the u a generous, continuous bottom turn, and keep its right stem and terminal clear. The v should retain its straight diagonal construction, with a minimally blunted bottom point for equal apparent weight. Preserve the a's enclosed lower counter and open upper aperture; enlarge those spaces slightly if needed for reduction.

Resolve spacing by the visible white intervals, not the original font cells. Keep a distinct breath between M and l, then let l, u, v, and a form an even, gently flowing rhythm. The l must not read as part of a ligature, and the u-v pair must not become a w. Use one baseline, one weight, opaque dark ink, and actual transparent alpha. Supply only this exact wordmark. No symbol, tagline, punctuation, alternate spelling, specimen label, background, lighting, or mockup belongs in its generated image.

## Soft Turn

[Open the Figma study](https://www.figma.com/design/4mdtod74gknaCCm8q1nrDj?node-id=151-1672)

### App identity

Mluva makes speaking feel like a natural way to begin useful written work. On an Omarchy desktop, a shortcut opens a small moment of capture; that speech becomes editable text, and an optional rewrite can help give it structure. Its identity should carry the ease of that transition and the confidence of being able to return to the original recognition. The app supports local and cloud choices independently for speech and rewriting, so the brand expresses user agency without implying that every operation stays on the device. Markdown, saved versions and retained source make the result feel like material the user owns and can continue working with. The tone is calm, direct and quietly pleasurable. “Most delightful dictation for Omarchy” is the product promise; “A little more delight every day” describes the cumulative feeling. The mark should belong beside practical desktop tools while suggesting a gentler entry into writing.

### Mark identity

Soft Turn gives the existing idea of an unfolding thought a more readable destination: a page. One compact filled shape contains a broad upper fold, an upright lower plane and a short speech-like extension at the lower left. A generous diagonal opening enters from the right, making the turn visible through genuine negative space. The two areas remain connected by a substantial left hinge. This is a graphic gesture of speech settling into something editable, rather than a diagram of the application's stages. The retained connection can support the story of keeping the original alongside revisions, but the logo does not claim to communicate that feature without explanation. Its upper portion feels open and conversational; its lower portion feels settled and usable. The balance between soft outer corners and one deliberate angular opening makes it both approachable and precise. It remains an abstract app emblem at a glance, with a speech-to-page reading available on a second look.

### Logo design

Build a compact, nearly square emblem from a single contiguous filled silhouette. Its footprint should feel more like a thick sheet caught in one modest turn than a document icon. The top is somewhat narrower than the lower portion. A broad, softly squared upper shoulder runs toward the right and then descends to the outer tip of the fold. Below it, a more upright lower plane supplies a calm right edge and an almost level base. The left side joins these regions in one continuous, substantial mass. Keep the overall stance upright; the interior diagonal supplies enough movement without rotating the whole mark.

The defining feature is a wide open notch entering from the right at approximately the upper two-fifths of the height. It travels diagonally down and left at roughly twenty-five degrees, ending just left of the optical center. Give this inner end a short, blunt return rather than a needle point. The notch separates two broad areas of the same opaque ink and opens directly into the transparent background. Its shape implies one turning layer without using a drawn crease, a second color or a shadow. The upper area should remain a substantial flap, not a thin ribbon. The lower area should occupy slightly more visual mass so the emblem settles rather than flies.

At the lower left, allow the base to resolve into a short, blunt, down-left extension. This is the only speech cue. It should emerge naturally from the contour and project less than one-tenth of the total height. Avoid a long chat-bubble pointer: the mark must stay compact, and the page-like plane should remain the principal reading. Use modest corner softening on exposed shoulders and the lower right, while preserving the diagonal opening as a clearly intentional cut.

Render the shape in one fully opaque ink on actual transparent alpha. There is no enclosing tile, outline, ruled text, paper texture, folded dog-ear, perspective shading or decorative perforation. At twenty-four pixels, the notch must read as a deliberate opening and the left hinge as solid material. At large size, the same silhouette should reward inspection through proportion and optical balance. The intended result is a quiet, tactile emblem whose meaning comes from one clear physical gesture: a thought turning into a usable page.

### Wordmark design

Set the exact name “Mluva”, with an uppercase M and lowercase luva. Begin with JetBrains Mono Medium outlines so the lettering belongs naturally to the application and film typography. Preserve the font's readable construction and practical character, then make a compact bespoke word rather than retaining equal character cells. The wordmark should feel composed and steady beside the folded symbol. It should not imitate paper folding across every letter or ask the reader to decode an experimental alphabet.

Keep the M upright and generous enough to establish the name immediately. Its interior diagonals should remain open at small sizes; soften their optical joins only enough to prevent dark clots. Narrow the l visibly, retaining a short entry flag and a modest right-facing foot so it cannot become an uppercase I. The foot may finish in a shallow chamfer related to the mark's twenty-five-degree opening. This is a quiet detail for larger artwork, not a feature on which recognition depends. Keep the u's bowl open and its right stem unambiguous. Give the v a clean inner valley and slightly blunt its lowest point to balance the softened symbol. Preserve a clearly double-storey a; open its counter and avoid a terminal that looks like a second fold or arrow.

Use optical spacing rather than a tracking value applied to all pairs. Close the M–l gap enough that the narrow l belongs to the word, let l–u breathe around the l foot, and slightly tighten the diagonal white space between u–v and v–a. These are starting directions for outline review, not approved numeric kerns. Maintain an even baseline and a comfortable lowercase height, around three-quarters of the M cap height. The word should read as one rhythm, with no exaggerated gap after the first letter.

Match the emblem through substantial ink, restrained corner softening and one consistent diagonal detail. Export the wordmark alone in the same solid monochrome on transparent alpha. Preserve font provenance when real outlines are produced. No symbol, tagline, border, underline, alternate spelling or background belongs in this asset. Final acceptance requires an exact five-letter read and a visual check of the narrowed l.
